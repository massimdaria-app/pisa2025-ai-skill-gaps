# Finding 02 — heterogeneity across modes of AI use (23 Oct 2026)

## Result

Across all six education systems, the frequency with which 15-year-olds
report using AI chatbots for schoolwork rises with family socio-economic
status (ESCS). This holds for every mode of use elicited in ST438.

The gradients, however, are not uniform. Pooled across the six countries,
the within-country ESCS gradient (Q5 minus Q1, weighted means) is:

| Mode of use            | Q1    | Q5    | Gap   |
|------------------------|-------|-------|-------|
| Draft my writing       | 2.43  | 2.55  | +0.12 |
| Summarise a text       | 2.48  | 2.78  | +0.30 |
| Preliminary research   | 2.59  | 3.06  | +0.47 |
| Help me learn          | 2.90  | 3.38  | +0.49 |

One mode stands apart. Using AI to draft written assignments — the mode
most directly substituting for the student's own work — is distributed
almost evenly across the socio-economic distribution. Its gradient is
roughly a quarter of that for the remaining three modes, which are
similar to one another.

The pattern is not driven by a single country. Drafting is flat in the
United Arab Emirates (3.23 to 3.22), Kazakhstan (2.97 to 2.98) and the
United Kingdom (2.07 to 2.08), and declines at the top quintile in
Brazil (2.62 at Q4 to 2.53 at Q5). It rises materially only in Australia
(2.24 to 2.71) and Spain (2.58 to 2.82). The learning mode, by contrast,
rises in all six systems, from +0.16 in the United Kingdom to +0.84 in
Australia.

## Interpretation

Substitutive use of AI appears to be socio-economically neutral: students
from the least advantaged quintile outsource written work at close to the
same rate as their most advantaged peers. What differs by background is
every other way of engaging with the technology.

This runs counter to the framing that dominates public debate on AI in
schools, which is organised almost entirely around academic dishonesty.
On these data, dishonesty is the one behaviour that does not stratify.

## Caveats

Descriptive only; no causal claim is made or implied. Self-reported
frequency on a five-point ordinal scale is averaged as if continuous.
Standard errors are not yet computed, so differences of a few hundredths
— including the apparent decline in drafting at Q5 in three countries —
should not be interpreted. Final student weights are applied; plausible
values and BRR replicate weights are not yet used, as no achievement
outcome enters this analysis.

Data: PISA 2025 student questionnaire, public use file. Six countries:
ARE, AUS, BRA, ESP, GBR, KAZ. N = 147,629 students, of whom approximately
130,000 answered the ST438 block.
